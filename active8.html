Active8 Planet

A speculative card game that puts the planet back into the room when agile teams make mobility decisions.

The challenge

Sustainability targets rarely translate into what agile teams actually feel day-to-day. The brief was to make the planet's stake in mobility decisions tangible enough for teams to act on, across industry, academia, and agile practice.

Process

Working from the Double Diamond and speculative/critical design methods, I researched sustainability challenges through interviews and literature, then translated the findings into design artefacts built to provoke reflection rather than just inform.

  1. Discovery. Interviews and literature review to map where sustainability thinking actually breaks down inside agile mobility teams.
  2. Speculative ideation. Used critical design methods to reframe abstract planetary impact as concrete, felt consequences of everyday decisions.
  3. Prototype & pilot. Built the card game format, ran pilot workshops, and iterated the mechanics based on what actually landed with participants.
  4. Digitise. Rebuilt select parts of the experience digitally so it could run beyond a single facilitated session.

Key decisions

  • A game, not a deck. The brief could have shipped as a slide deck or report. I chose a game because the goal was to make teams feel the consequences of a decision, not just read about them.
  • Speculative framing. Asking teams to imagine a world without cars was a deliberate provocation: abstract sustainability data rarely moves people, but a confronted assumption does.
  • Pilot before scale. Rather than finalising the format up front, I treated the first workshops as tests, changing pacing and card mechanics based on where participants disengaged.

Outcome

The result was a card-based training game for agile teams, visualising the downstream planetary cost of everyday decisions. I led the design and facilitation of pilot workshops to test and iterate the concept, then digitised parts of it for a more dynamic, repeatable experience. Participants moved from discussing sustainability in the abstract to naming specific decisions in their own workflow they'd reconsider: the clearest sign the format was doing its job.

Reflection

Watching participants question core assumptions about mobility, after being asked to imagine a world without cars, showed me how far a well-built design tool can push a group past its defaults. It sharpened how I use creative methods to make abstract, value-laden topics discussable.
